All work Case study · St. Roch Market

Vendor settlements went from six hours a week to about ten minutes.

I operate St. Roch Market: eleven independent vendors and a bar under one roof, in a building that has been trading food since 1875. Every Monday, each vendor needs to get paid the exact right amount. This is the system that does it.

The state before

Each vendor has their own commission rate, delivery fee split, daily minimum, carry-over balance, and a pile of linen and supply adjustments. The reconciliation lived in Google Sheets, stitched to Square exports by hand, after closing, with a tired brain.

It took about six hours a week and it had to be perfect, because these are the numbers a vendor gets paid on. Get them wrong and you lose vendor trust, and vendor trust is the operation.

What I built

Food Hall Manager pulls live sales data from Square at vendor level, applies each vendor's actual rules, and produces a settlement statement that explains itself. Every line item traces back to the Square transaction it came from.

  • Live Square sync at vendor level, no manual exports
  • Commission structures set per vendor: tiered, flat, or hybrid
  • Delivery fee splits, daily minimums, and supply charges as line items
  • Balance carry-over between weeks with a full audit trail
  • CPA-ready Excel exports for month end and year end
  • A vendor portal where each vendor reviews their own numbers in real time

What changed

Settlements run on a button, and a human reviews for about ten minutes instead of rebuilding spreadsheets for six hours. Vendors stopped emailing to ask if their number is right. They log in and see the answer, down to the line items. When a number gets disputed, the audit log answers in one search.

The Manager Portal runs beside it for the physical side of the building: shift handoff notes, opening and closing checklists, bar inventory with par alerts, and ordering built from what actually sold.

What I'd do differently

Three things I got wrong and fixed the hard way.

Underestimated the edge cases

The first commission engine handled the clean cases and broke on the vendors with custom hybrid deals. The rewrite around per-vendor rule sets should have been the day-one design.

Built the vendor portal too late

For six months I ran settlements without giving vendors their own view, and answered "is my number right?" by email. The portal ended those emails immediately. It should have shipped with version one.

Audit logging came second

Before the audit trail existed, reconstructing a disputed number was painful. Now every state change is logged from the start, on everything I build.

Operating a food hall or multi-vendor space?

The rules engine is built and the patterns generalize. Your vendor deals go in free, whatever shape they're in.

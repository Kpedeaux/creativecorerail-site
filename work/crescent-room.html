The booking system that ended a monthly software fee.

CR Coffee rents out the Crescent Room, a 12-seat meeting room on Magazine Street. Booking it used to run through Square Appointments, which costs a monthly fee. Now CoreRail sells the room, takes the payment, and tells the staff on shift the moment it happens.

The fee

The Crescent Room is good business: two and three hour rentals with drip coffee included, plus a coffee-and-pastry package for meetings. But the booking flow lived in Square Appointments, a subscription that charged every month for what amounts to a calendar, a payment, and a notification.

That's the pattern with small business software. Each need gets its own tool, each tool gets its own login, and each login gets its own monthly fee.

What I built

Booking moved into CoreRail, the same system the baristas already use for schedules and checklists. The public page shows the room, the three packages, and live availability. A customer picks a time and pays online.

  • The customer books and pays on a public page, no account needed
  • The booking lands on the CR Coffee events calendar automatically
  • The baristas scheduled that day get a message the moment it books
  • Sales tax, receipts, and payouts flow through Square like every other sale

What changed

The subscription is canceled. Nobody copies bookings between systems, and nobody gets surprised by a meeting walking in at 9 a.m. The people opening the shop that morning already knew: their phones told them when the booking happened.

This is the part I'd point at if you run a small business: the win isn't just the fee. It's that the sale, the calendar, and the staff all found out at the same time, from one system.

Paying monthly for something this simple?

Booking, gift cards, waitlists, forms: a lot of subscriptions can become software you own.
